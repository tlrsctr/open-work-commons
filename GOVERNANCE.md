# Bootstrap governance

This charter governs the pre-community bootstrap period and expires six months after the first public release unless replaced through the public RFC process.

## Mission lock

The project exists to keep human-and-agent work locally operable, portable, inspectable, verifiable, and replaceable. Sponsor funding, employment, or hosting volume confers no authority over protocol behavior or user data.

## Decisions

- Normative behavior changes through public RFCs with use cases, non-goals, privacy and threat analysis, wire/schema changes, compatibility impact, migration, and conformance fixtures.
- Routine implementation decisions use review by the maintainers responsible for that package.
- Disputed normative decisions remain unresolved rather than being silently chosen by an implementation.
- Governance, mission, or license changes require at least 30 days of public review and a recorded supermajority decision once a technical steering committee exists.

## Contributions

Contributions use DCO 1.1 sign-off. There is no copyright assignment. Maintainer roles must have documented scope, review expectations, inactivity rules, and removal procedures before the first public release.

## Neutral stewardship trigger

When the project has active maintainers from at least three independent organizations and two independent conforming implementations, its trademarks and domains should transfer to a neutral nonprofit, foundation, or cooperative steward under a publicly reviewed agreement.

## Data rights

User content is not project data and is never automatically open data. Reference software must provide documented export, deletion, retention, permission, and hosting choices. Model training on user content requires separate explicit consent.
